"My background is in audit and independent analysis. I bring that same discipline to every long-term care engagement — no carrier bias, no promotional agenda, and no recommendation that I cannot justify analytically."

Why Long-Term Care Planning

Over 20 years ago, Bert recognized that long-term care risk represented one of the most significant unaddressed financial exposures in retirement planning — particularly for professionals who had done everything else correctly. Market risk was managed. Estate plans were in place. But the potential financial impact of a prolonged care event was frequently unquantified and uninsured.

His accounting background made him well-suited to approach the problem analytically. The question was not "which product should I recommend?" but "what is the actual financial exposure, and what is the most rational structure to address it for this specific client?" That question has driven every engagement since.

The Independent Advantage

Most long-term care insurance professionals are either captive agents — representing a single carrier — or have deeply preferred carriers that drive the majority of their recommendations. Independent analysis is structurally different: it begins with the client's financial profile, health status, and planning objectives, and identifies which structures and carriers are genuinely most appropriate before any recommendation is made.

This distinction becomes particularly important in a market where product structures, carrier pricing, financial strength, and claim service quality vary substantially. A recommendation grounded in comprehensive comparison is materially more defensible than one anchored to a single relationship.

Who He Works With

Bert's practice is focused on professionals, executives, and their families in the San Francisco Bay Area and Northern California — individuals who approach financial decisions analytically and who want a consultative engagement rather than a sales experience.

His clients include CPAs, attorneys, physicians, financial executives, and business owners — professionals who value independent analysis and are sophisticated enough to evaluate the underlying logic of a recommendation, not just the summary conclusion.
