The Planning Imperative

Why Long-Term Care Planning Matters

This is not an emotional argument. These are financial facts that belong in any rigorous retirement analysis.

01

Medicare Does Not Cover Extended Care

Medicare is designed for acute and rehabilitative care. It provides limited coverage for skilled nursing and no meaningful coverage for custodial long-term care — the type most families will need.

02

Bay Area Costs Exceed National Averages

Professional home care and memory care facilities in the San Francisco Bay Area routinely exceed $150,000 per person annually — costs that compound significantly over a multi-year event.

03

Dementia Events Can Last a Decade or More

Dementia-related care is not a short-term event. The average duration exceeds five years, and many cases extend well beyond ten, creating sustained financial exposure unlike most insurable risks.

04

Most Care Occurs at Home

The majority of long-term care today is delivered in the home, not in nursing facilities. Benefit structures that are limited to facility-based care may significantly underperform in practice.

05

Women Statistically Require Care Longer

Women live longer on average and are statistically more likely to require extended long-term care. Couples planning that ignores this asymmetry carries meaningful actuarial risk.

06

Self-Insuring Carries Hidden Costs

High-net-worth families often assume they can self-insure against LTC risk. An unstructured care event can erode investment portfolios, disrupt income strategies, and transfer disproportionate burden to family members.

Planning Strategies

Sophisticated Solutions for Complex Needs

No single carrier or product is appropriate for every client. Independent analysis across multiple structures produces meaningfully better outcomes.

Strategy 01

Hybrid Life & LTC

Combines a life insurance death benefit with long-term care protection. Premiums are guaranteed. If care is never needed, a benefit passes to heirs.

Strategy 02

Asset-Based LTC Planning

Repositions existing assets — often low-yield or underperforming holdings — to fund long-term care costs on a leveraged, tax-advantaged basis.

Strategy 03

Lifetime Benefit Designs

Structured for potentially open-ended care events. Provides protection against the longest and most costly care scenarios, including advanced dementia.

Strategy 04

Executive Protection Strategies

Designed specifically for high-income professionals. Addresses income replacement, business continuity, and coordination with deferred compensation structures.

Strategy 05

Multi-Carrier Comparative Analysis

Independent evaluation of leading carriers across benefit structures, financial strength, claim experience, and long-term pricing stability. No single carrier is best for everyone.
